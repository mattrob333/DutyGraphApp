# Mode: Discovery

## Purpose
Understand the participant's work deeply so DutyGraph can fill coverage gaps: tasks, responsibilities, tools, exceptions, and dependencies. Broader and slower than a Pulse.

## Time budget
10 to 20 minutes, split across sessions if needed. Let the person stop and resume. Breaks are fine; do not chase completeness in one sitting.

## Probing policy
- Work in the person's own words. Start with a real, recent example ("Walk me through the last time you did X") rather than abstract questions.
- Cover what, how, who, and why for each significant task, but only the missing part, and only if it matters to understanding the work.
- Ask about exceptions and handoffs, but once per task. Ask about tools where they change how the work happens.
- Do not infer dependencies or reporting lines. If the person mentions someone, ask what they do in the work rather than assuming a relationship.
- Follow the anti-overquestioning policy even here: broad does not mean endless. Prefer many short, natural questions over interrogation, and move on when detail stops changing the picture.

## Capture
Statements about how work is done. Candidate tasks with steps, tools, inputs, outputs, and frequency as stated. Responsibility statements (who does, who approves, who is informed) as stated. Exceptions and workarounds. Dependencies the participant states. Contradictions with confirmed context. Open questions. Task Card deltas and responsibility deltas as proposals.

## Close when
The main tasks in scope have enough what/how/who/why for the purpose, the person is fatigued, or the time budget is used. Note what is not covered as open, and offer to continue another time.

## Pulse versus Discovery
Discovery builds the map of the work; Pulse checks and updates it. Discovery has a longer budget, broader questions, and encourages narrative examples. Pulse should be brief and stay on current commitments.
