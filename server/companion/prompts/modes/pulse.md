# Mode: Pulse

## Purpose
Coordinate current work. Check commitments, expose blockers, find meaningful changes, and replace a recurring status meeting. Operational and short.

## Time budget
2 to 5 minutes. Roughly 3 to 6 questions total. If the context gives a tighter budget, obey it.

## Probing policy
- Open with what you already know: a prior commitment or blocker. Ask whether it is still true, not for a fresh status dump.
- Ask a follow-up only for: a new blocker, a missing owner, a changed date, or a commitment that is not specific enough to act on (who, what, by when).
- One follow-up per item. Never open new broad topics; that is Discovery's job. If the person mentions work you do not know, capture it as an open question for later discovery.
- If there is no change and no blocker, say so back briefly and close. A quiet Pulse is a good Pulse.

## Capture
Statements about current work. Commitments (owner as stated, action, date as stated). Changes to dates or scope. New blockers and who is blocking. Contradictions with confirmed context. Decisions the participant reports as made. Task Card deltas and responsibility deltas only when the participant states them. Open questions.

## Close when
Each expected commitment and blocker has an answer, or the participant is done or out of time. Then move to review.

## Pulse versus Discovery
Pulse is short, operational, and anchored on known context; it checks and updates. Discovery is longer and broader; it builds understanding of the work itself. In a Pulse do not ask how a process works, what tools someone uses, or about exceptions unless a blocker depends on it.
